Never navigated an offshore race? Start here

Offshore navigation is a real discipline — reading forecasts, picking a lane, trading distance for wind. This is the lowest-stakes way there is to learn it: the boat is virtual, the ocean isn't.

  1. Register (free, below) and open a race that's upcoming or in progress, then register a boat on its page.
  2. No software needed to play: hit Draw on chart, click a line of waypoints from start to finish, submit. You're racing. A straight line is a legitimate first strategy — the leaderboard will teach you why the winners didn't sail one.
  3. Look at the weather once a day. Download the race's on-board forecast (a GRIB file — the same wind your boat will sail) and adjust the part of your route you haven't sailed yet. Position, forecast, tweak: that's the whole navigator's loop.
  4. Level up with free tools. qtVlm is free routing software the pros practice on: import the race's course route and polar, load the GRIB, let it compute an optimal route, export GPX, upload here. Now you're weather routing like a Vendée navigator.
  5. Compare your wake. Your sailed track downloads as GPX — overlay it on the real fleet's and see exactly where the ocean agreed with you.

Account

Navigators enter races and submit routings. The first account on a fresh server becomes the admin; admins create races (and race too — their profile is a navigator profile like any other).

The rules of the game

  1. One polar for the whole fleet, derated to a realistic performance factor — nobody wins on boatspeed, only on routing.
  2. The engine sails your waypoints through real surface wind and current every few minutes, steering best-VMC toward your next mark — a wind shift never parks you head-to-wind.
  3. Updates replace only the future. The past is locked, tacks and gybes cost seconds, water under 15 ft costs half your speed, and a routing that misses a mark gets bent back through it — never disqualified, never shortened.
  4. Your future route is private until sailed; your wake is public, like any race tracker.

⚡ Auto-create from the Notice of Race / Sailing Instructions

Drop in the official race documents (PDF or text) and a virtual race is created automatically — name, start time, and course marks are read from the documents, which are attached to the race page for competitors. Marks are only taken from positions stated in the documents, never guessed. AI document reading needs Anthropic credentials on the server; otherwise a pattern parser handles documents with coordinate tables.

    Connecting your navigation software
    
    
    Getting the polar in
    
    
    Each race page offers the polar as a .pol text file
    (TWA rows × TWS columns) — the format read by Expedition, qtVlm, OpenCPN
    weather_routing, Adrena and most others. Apply the race performance factor
    if you want your routing to match the engine exactly.
    
    
    Getting the course in
    
    
    Every race page offers the official marks as a course route (.gpx) —
    import it into your software, route between the marks with your GRIBs, and
    the exported result is your first submission.
    
    
    Getting your routing out
    
    
    Export the optimal route as GPX (universally supported) or CSV with
    lat/lon columns, and upload it on the race page. Only the waypoints matter —
    the engine sails them in order at polar speed through real wind.
    
    
    Getting your boat back in
    
    
    Download your sailed track as GPX from the race page and overlay it in your
    software against the GRIBs to see where your routing won or lost.
    
    
    Real fleet
    
    
    Race admins can import tracker positions for the real boats (GPX track or
    CSV time,lat,lon — e.g. exported from YB/Yellowbrick race viewers)
    and tag each with a class label, so the leaderboard shows how you stack up
    against the boats actually sailing your polar.
